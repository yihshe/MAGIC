# Physical consistency loss added to PILA (rebuttal)

> **Reviewer.** "Could PILA potentially be improved through addition of a physical
> consistency loss as in HVAE?"

Run on **GreenHyperSpectra**, so the answer is a per-trait error against measured
traits rather than an assertion. Data and evaluator are shared with
`rebuttal/greenhyperspectra/`, which is **read-only** here.

**Answer: it does not help.** All three weights land within ±1% of the
no-consistency baseline, and the only weight large enough to matter during
training makes it slightly *worse*. See [Results](#results).

## Design

HVAE's physical-consistency term is the synthetic-pair inversion loss: sample
`z ~ U(0,1)`, push it through the physics model, require the encoder to invert it
back, and compare the **inferred z against the sampled z**. PILA gets exactly that
comparison (`PhysVAETrainerSMPL._synthetic_consistency_loss_z`); the only
difference from HVAE is where the squashing sits — HVAE's encoder head ends in
Softplus and emits z directly, PILA's emits u, so `draw`'s sigmoid is applied
before comparing.

PILA's pre-existing `_synthetic_data_loss` (u against `logit(z)`) is the Stage-A
bootstrap; it is left untouched and is inactive at `epochs_pretrain: 0`.

**No baseline is trained here** — the weight-0 reference is the existing
`PILA_PROSAIL_C_GHS` run under `saved/greenhyperspectra/`.

| run | weight |
|---|---|
| `S1_w0.1` | 0.1 |
| `S2_w1.0` | 1.0 |
| `S3_w10` | 10.0 |

## On the weights (and the 1721 bands)

The band count does **not** need compensating for: this loss never touches the
spectral axis, and `rec_loss` is already normalised per band via
`trainer.rec_loss_scale`.

The weights were chosen from measurement. In z-space the term is a sum of squared
errors over 4 latents in [0,1], so it is small in absolute terms — ~0.3 at the end
of the first epoch, against `rec_loss` ~6.4. (PILA's u-space Stage-A bootstrap
regresses u against `logit(z)`, whose targets have `var = π²/3`; that version is
~30x larger, so weights calibrated for it would be ~30x too small here.) The three
weights were picked to span "negligible", "the order of magnitude at which HVAE
keeps its own regularisers", and "large enough to visibly compete with
reconstruction".

What each weight actually bought, measured from the training logs
(`figures/loss_balance.csv`, regenerated by `summary_consistency_loss.py`):

| weight | consistency term | `rec_loss` | **share of `rec_loss`** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.367 → 0.040 | 6.41 → 0.88 | **0.6% → 0.5%** |
| 1.0 | 0.317 → 0.018 | 6.44 → 0.88 | **4.9% → 2.0%** |
| 10 | 0.176 → 0.009 | 5.80 → 0.89 | **30.4% → 9.5%** |

(first epoch → last epoch; share is `weight x consistency / rec_loss`.)

**The term extinguishes itself.** The consistency loss falls ~20x over training —
the encoder learns to invert synthetic pairs quickly, because inverting a clean,
noise-free physics simulation is a much easier problem than inverting real
spectra. So even at weight 10, which starts out contributing 30% of the loss, the
term has decayed to under 10% by the end and is no longer shaping the solution.
That is the mechanistic reason the accuracy barely moves at any weight, and it is
worth stating in the rebuttal alongside the numbers: the term is not being
crowded out by a bad weight choice, it is simply easy to satisfy and therefore
uninformative about real spectra.

## Results

Mean prediction (physical units, over the labelled rows) and MAE on the
range-normalized residual, i.e. the convention in `visuals/analysis_rtm_insitu.py`.
Generated by `summary_consistency_loss.py` into `figures/mean_mae_table.csv`.

| Model | cab Mean | cab MAE | cw Mean | cw MAE | cm Mean | cm MAE | LAI Mean | LAI MAE | mean MAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PILA (no consistency)** | 40.74 | 0.113 | 0.02147 | 0.119 | 0.01574 | 0.134 | 2.075 | 0.170 | **0.134** |
| PILA + consistency w=0.1 | 40.47 | 0.110 | 0.02144 | 0.119 | 0.01555 | 0.132 | 2.082 | 0.170 | 0.133 |
| PILA + consistency w=1.0 | 40.57 | 0.112 | 0.02167 | 0.122 | 0.01557 | 0.132 | 2.076 | 0.170 | 0.134 |
| PILA + consistency w=10 | 40.53 | 0.113 | 0.02171 | 0.121 | 0.01559 | 0.134 | 2.082 | 0.173 | 0.135 |
| Ground Truth | 37.84 | N/A | 0.01392 | N/A | 0.008714 | N/A | 3.247 | N/A | N/A |

n per trait: cab=396, cw=424, cm=736, LAI=284.

Change in mean MAE against the no-consistency baseline:

| weight | mean MAE | change |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.1331 | −0.0009 (−0.7%) |
| 1.0 | 0.1340 | −0.0001 (−0.0%) |
| 10 | 0.1352 | **+0.0012 (+0.9%)** |

No trait improves by more than 0.003 at any weight, and the direction is
**non-monotone in the weight** — best at 0.1, worst at 10 — which is the signature
of noise rather than an effect. The largest weight, the only one that materially
enters the loss, is the one that degrades accuracy.

One honesty caveat for the write-up: with a single seed per weight there is no
variance estimate on GHS, so "no significant effect" rests on the effect size
being tiny and sign-flipping, not on a measured spread. The Austria seed sweep
(`rebuttal/seed_variance/`) gives a sense of how large run-to-run variation is in
this pipeline.

**What the spread adds.** The result is not "we picked a bad weight": the sweep
covers 0.5% to 30% of `rec_loss` and accuracy is flat across all of it. So the
honest claim is the stronger one — the term is inert here, and adding it would
buy PILA a second loss weight to carry and justify for no measured return, which
is exactly the multi-term balancing burden PILA was designed to avoid.

**Caveat for the write-up.** Matching synthetic pairs is not supervision. Where
measured labels exist, semi-supervised training on the traits themselves is the
better use of them; this experiment answers the question only as the reviewer
posed it.

## Commands

`run_consistency_loss.sh` is a menu, not a driver: every command in it is
commented out. Open it and uncomment the step you want.

```bash
python rebuttal/consistency_loss/make_configs.py
python train_pila.py --config rebuttal/consistency_loss/configs/PILA_PROSAIL_C_GHS_S1_w0.1.json
# ... S2_w1.0, S3_w10 likewise — ~1.7 h each, independent
python rebuttal/greenhyperspectra/eval_greenhyperspectra.py \
    --resume saved/rebuttal/consistency_loss/<name>/<run>/models/model_best.pth \
    --out_dir rebuttal/consistency_loss/figures
python rebuttal/compare_ghs_variants.py \
    --dir rebuttal/consistency_loss/figures --baseline PILA_PROSAIL_C_GHS

# the MAE table + the measured loss balance behind the weight choice
python rebuttal/consistency_loss/summary_consistency_loss.py
```

Requires the GHS dataset built by
`rebuttal/greenhyperspectra/preprocess_greenhyperspectra.py`. Each run is scored
with `rebuttal/greenhyperspectra/eval_greenhyperspectra.py` into `figures/`.
A 2-epoch smoke config (`configs/_debug_*.json`) runs the path in ~2 min. Long
runs die with their parent shell — see the `setsid` note at the top of the script.

## Code changes

One knob in `trainer/trainer_phys_smpl.py`, marked `[REBUTTAL ONLY]`:

- `balance_data_aug_cotrain` (default `0.0`) — enables
  `_synthetic_consistency_loss_z` during the main phase. At 0.0 the term is never
  computed, so every config under `configs/` keeps the published code path.

Verified: with the published Austria config, PILA reproduces its pre-change
training losses **bit-identically** over 2 epochs; the loss arithmetic of the new
term was checked by hand against the logs.
